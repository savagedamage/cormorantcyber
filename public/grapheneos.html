Same phone, five days apart

What a Pixel looks like on stock Android versus GrapheneOS — same device, same user, measured with the same read-only collectors on both sides. No assumptions: every number below was pulled from the device itself.

device: Pixel 8 (same unit, both exams) stock exam: 2026-09-11 · grapheneos: flash day + 5 days of real use stock figures are post-hardening floors

The numbers

Full device exams, three states of the same phone. The stock column is a floor, not a fresh-device picture: the user had already revoked permissions by hand before collection, so a truly untouched stock phone is worse than what you see here.

Read the stock column honestly: it was collected after the user had already hardened the phone by hand — revocations, standby buckets, disables. It is the best a stock phone gets without fighting the OS. And it still had 84 camera grants.

What "acting" looked like on each side

The same user, the same habits, the same collectors. The difference is entirely in what the operating system allows by default.

Stock Android — the floor

100+ packages that could reach the mic or camera

84 packages could open the camera, 30 the microphone, 7 SMS. The access logs showed hundreds of mic and camera events a month, most from apps with no business touching either — a VPN with 10 mic events, a calendar app with 16 camera events, a browser with 12 camera events including one in the background. A remote-support agent sat in the OS as a system app. Every one of those surfaces is "legitimate" individually, which is exactly the problem: nothing looks like spyware, and that's why nobody notices.

GrapheneOS — after five days of real use

The ceiling becomes the user's own decisions

The user reinstalled their whole life — messaging, mail, wallets, AI assistants, a terminal, automation agents, a scanner, two VPNs, radio tools: 74 apps. Camera grants: 11, every one an app whose job involves a lens or a QR code. Mic: 5, every one a voice feature the user turned on — plus one scanner app holding a mic grant it didn't need, flagged and revoked (its entire usage history: one 4.8-second access). In 26 hours of observation the camera opened exactly three times. Zero background camera events. No OEM telemetry. The only remote agent on the device is one the owner installed themselves — and the OS can throttle it when idle.

The difference isn't willpower. The stock phone got the same hardening pass and still had 84 camera grants, because on stock the OS itself and the OEM apps own the permissions. On GrapheneOS the floor is zero and the ceiling is the user's own choices.

Why the numbers matter — and what they don't claim

  • This was never a spyware hunt: the stock exam matched 0 of 613 stalkerware indicators. The real finding is a consent and transparency problem — 100+ packages that can reach the mic/camera with no user-facing indication, most without a meaningful opt-out.
  • The GrapheneOS numbers are honest: grants grow back as the user re-grants them. What stays zero is the OEM layer — the 50 bloat packages, the remote-support agent, the Play-Services-grabs-the-mic-by-default behavior.
  • Five days is a floor, not a ceiling: behavior ledgers only fill over months. The 26-hour camera log is the observed evidence, and it's presented as exactly that.

What this means if you carry a phone

  1. You don't need a "spyware infection" to be watched. Default-granted, OEM-level permission surfaces do the job without any malware.
  2. The single highest-leverage upgrade is the OS, not another privacy app. A Pixel running GrapheneOS starts from zero: no carrier apps, no remote-support backdoor, Google optional and sandboxed.
  3. Your apps still ask for what they ask — that part is on you. But on GrapheneOS a bad grant is your decision, not the manufacturer's.
